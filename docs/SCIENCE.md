# Red Planet: the science

Every number the mod uses, where it comes from, and what it does in the game. Where the game departs from reality on
purpose, the real value sits next to the game value and the reason is given (§21 lists all such compromises).

- **Sources.** Short citations below. Full citations, URLs and verification notes are in the fact-check,
  [api-notes/science-sources.md](api-notes/science-sources.md) (cited as *FC §n*). Items checked afterwards are in
  [api-notes/science-extra.md](api-notes/science-extra.md) (*FX §n*). A few textbook constants and
  pointers that were not re-read are marked *pointer*.
- **Kinds of value.** *Measured*: a published measurement. *Computed*: derived from measured constants (formulas in
  FC appendix or here). *Model*: a model built for the mod and calibrated against measurements (calibration shown).
  *Compromise*: deliberately changed for gameplay (§21).
- **Science and fiction.** The planet is science: terrain, sky, clock, gravity, air, temperature, weather,
  radiation and the rocket follow this document. What lives on Mars and what is buried there (native cave life,
  the Arean ruins and vaults, the creatures and the twin guardians, DESIGN.md §8) is fiction and is labelled as
  such. The fiction obeys the physics: nothing native breathes oxygen or burns, everything falls at Mars gravity,
  and life hides underground from the radiation. No life has been confirmed on Mars; Perseverance's "Sapphire
  Canyon" sample (2025) holds potential biosignatures still under study (NASA, *pointer*).
- **Where numbers live in code.** Planet parameters are environment attributes in
  `data/redplanet/dimension_type/mars.json`, generated by `tools/datagen/gen_mars_data.py`. Formulas are in
  `mars/astro/` (calendar, astronomy, climate), `mars/geo/` (map), `environment/AtmosphereModel`,
  `starship/geometry/StarshipGeometry` and the flight profiles in `data/redplanet/flight_profile/`. Unit tests check
  the computed values quoted here.

## 4. Air drag

Minecraft's drag is linear: each tick, velocity is multiplied by a constant (0.98 vertically for living entities;
other values for items, arrows and so on). Real drag grows with the square of speed, so terminal speed scales as
√(g/ρ). The mod scales every per-tick drag *loss* by

> K = √(ρ_rel · g_rel)   (Earth: exactly 1; Mars: √(0.01135 × 0.3794) = **0.0656**)

For a linear drag model the terminal speed is g(1 − d)/d, so this choice reproduces the real ratio of terminal speeds,
√(g_rel/ρ_rel), while keeping vanilla's model, and leaves Earth untouched.

## 8. Temperature

`MarsClimate.surfaceTemperature` is a compact physical model of ground temperature:

1. **Daily mean**: radiative balance of the daily-mean insolation Q (latitude, solar declination, Sun distance):
   T = (Q(1 − A)/(εσ))^¼ + 5 K, with albedo A = 0.25 (TES mean bright/dark mix; ice-covered ground passes its own
   albedo) and emissivity ε = 0.95. The thin CO₂ atmosphere adds only a few kelvin of greenhouse warming.
2. **Diurnal cycle**: a skewed cosine peaking at 13:30 local time whose amplitude, 46 K × min(1.15, √(Q_noon/590)),
   grows with noon insolation and shrinks with dust optical depth (low thermal inertia gives ~90 K swings at Gale).
3. **CO₂ frost floor**: the ground can't cool below the CO₂ frost point at the local pressure (§7).

| Gale, late southern winter (Ls ~150–185), ground | max ≈286 K, min ≈187 K (REMS, before the 2018 storm) | Viúdez-Moreiras et al. 2019 (FC §5) | Ls 150: 269 / 196 K; Ls 180: 280 / 202 K | Diurnal range 73–78 K vs 94 K measured: slightly damped so the planet's hottest ground stays inside NASA's range (next row) |

## 21. Deliberate compromises

| Area | Reality | Game | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Map scale | 1:1 | 1 block = 1 km horizontally, 100 m vertically (10× vertical exaggeration on the equator) | A whole Mars you can travel; big landforms read as mountains and canyons |
| Polar map | Poles are points | Mercator, mirrored past ±85° | No wall at the map edge; craters stay round |
| Human scale | — | Rocket, player, boulders, dunes and dust devils at 1 block = 1 m | Minecraft is played at human scale |
| Time | 1 sol = 24.66 h | 20.55 min (72×, like vanilla) | Vanilla convention |
| Gravity | 3.72 m/s² absolute | 0.3794 × vanilla gravity | Vanilla gravity is already ~3.3 g |
| Drag | Quadratic | Vanilla's linear drag, loss scaled by √(ρg) | Correct terminal-speed ratio without changing Earth |
| Air control | — | Unchanged on Mars | Playability |
| Hypoxia | Unconscious at ~10–15 s; death after ~2 min | 15 s of air, then blackout effects and slow damage to death at ~115 s | Close to reality; Minecraft has no unconscious state |
| Sound | −20 dB | −10.5 dB plus low-pass | Playability |
| Liquid water | Metastable in the deepest basins | Evaporates everywhere outside habitats | Simplicity; it would evaporate quickly anyway |
| Weather | Regional storms at no single rate | Regional storms about every 30 sols in the dust season | Gameplay rate (configurable) |
| Dust devils | ~50 per km² per sol at Gusev | A few visible at a time | The human-scale map would otherwise be full of them |
| Radiation | Effects build over years | Dose tracked, effects off by default | Real doses cause no acute illness |
| Flight duration | Months | Minutes in the world; true mission time on the HUD | Playability, with true numbers on screen |
| Flight altitude | 68 km at staging, 200 km orbit | Logarithmic above 300 m | Keeps the rocket inside the loaded world |
| Transfer calendar | Arrive 6 months later | The shared world clock does not jump | Multiplayer worlds share one clock |
| Earth's orbit | e = 0.017 | Circular | ≤ 1° error in Earth's position in the Mars sky |
| Pressure | Weather-scale fluctuations, temperature-dependent scale height | Fixed scale height, smooth seasonal cycle | Small effect |
| Booster engines | 1.3 m nozzles | Outer ring drawn at 1.20 m | 20 nozzles fit inside the 9 m skirt |
| Habitat shielding | Structure-dependent | −30 % | Gameplay estimate |
